01 · Why

The problem with how schools communicate.

Why I built Navigator AI, and why it matters.

Driving question

Can a school-specific AI assistant make families' and students' lives meaningfully easier by turning scattered school communications into clear, on-demand answers?

School information at KLS is spread across many different systems. Announcements often come through ParentSquare and email, class information may live in Canvas, policies are stored in handbooks or documents, and other updates appear through separate tools. Each platform is useful on its own, but together they can make school information difficult to navigate. A student, parent, or teacher may know that an answer exists somewhere, but still have to search across old posts, messages, calendars, and documents to find it.

The problem I wanted to address was the gap between communication and usability. Schools send a lot of information, but that does not always mean people can easily act on it. A family might miss a deadline, a student might be unsure what changed, or a teacher might receive repeated questions about policies, schedules, forms, or course requirements. I took an initial input survey from teachers and staff, and their responses helped confirm that Navigator could be useful for areas such as academic policies, forms, course scheduling, and source-based answers. They also raised important concerns about correctness, audience-specific guidance, and how easily information could be updated.

Navigator AI grew out of this need. I wanted to contribute to improving the technology stack at KLS by building an intelligent layer that could sit above existing school communications and make them easier to search, understand, and verify. I have also had conversations with Laura about how this kind of tool could support school communication more broadly, especially if it is designed carefully and responsibly.

This project is meaningful to me because I have personally seen how much time families, students, and teachers lose to information overload. In my own family, we have had to search through old school communications to find deadlines, events, forms, or instructions. Navigator became a way for me to address a real school problem while learning what it takes to build technology that people could actually use.
