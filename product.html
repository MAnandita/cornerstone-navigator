What I created.

A working private chatbot for school communities — deployed and piloted at KLS.

Navigator AI is a working school information assistant designed to help students, families, teachers, and staff navigate school communications more easily. It turns school updates into a searchable, source-grounded system that can answer questions, highlight relevant deadlines and events, and help users understand information that would otherwise be scattered across multiple tools.

The main product surface is chat. A user can ask questions such as "What is happening this week?", "Are there any forms I need to complete?", "When is this event?", or "What did I miss recently?" Navigator searches the user's available school communications and generates a clear answer. Inspired by NotebookLM, answers include citations directly in the response, and users can click those citations to open the original source in a sidebar. This makes the answer easier to verify and helps users understand exactly where the information came from.

Navigator also includes a recent-updates view that helps users see upcoming events, deadlines, and important changes. There is a searchable posts archive where users can browse the underlying school communications directly. In the chat sidebar, users can also organize conversations into projects, making it easier to keep related questions or topics together, such as course planning, events, forms, or school policies.

Another major feature is translation and simplification. Users can translate responses into another language or ask for a simpler version of an answer. This matters because school communication should be accessible to families with different language backgrounds, time constraints, and levels of familiarity with school systems.

The broader purpose of Navigator is to explore how KLS's technology stack could become more connected, intelligent, and easier to use. Right now, school information moves through separate systems for communication, classes, calendars, and documents. Navigator is an initial step toward building a more unified layer on top of those systems: one that helps people search, verify, organize, and act on school information more effectively. By combining source-grounded AI, citations, translation, recent updates, projects, and user feedback, the product points toward a future where school technology is not just a collection of tools, but a more coherent system that supports the daily needs of the community.
